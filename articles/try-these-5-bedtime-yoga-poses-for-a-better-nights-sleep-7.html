5 Bedtime Yoga Poses for a Better Night's Sleep

By Dr. Lisa Park Senior Editor
Published: May 21, 2026 Updated: May 21, 2026 6 Min Read

Sleep quality is intrinsically linked to how we release physical tension at the end of the day. In our modern, high-stress lives, the body often remains in a "fight or flight" mode even when we lie down, leading to restless tossing and turning. Bedtime yoga serves as a bridge between the chaos of the day and the restorative peace of the night, helping us transition our nervous system from an active state into deep rest.

Unlike vigorous morning flows, bedtime yoga—often referred to as restorative yoga—is designed to be slow and grounding. It focuses on gentle stretches that target the areas where we hold the most stress: the hips, shoulders, and lower back. By practicing these five specific poses, you can lower your cortisol levels and signal to your brain that it is time to shut down.

Why Restorative Yoga Improves Sleep

Restorative yoga activates the parasympathetic nervous system, commonly known as the "rest and digest" mode. When you hold gentle stretches for several minutes, you encourage the muscles to release chronic tension. This release is often accompanied by a drop in heart rate and blood pressure, creating the ideal physiological environment for melatonin production.

Furthermore, the focus on deep, rhythmic breathing serves as a form of moving meditation. By shifting your attention from the stressors of the day to the rhythm of your breath, you effectively break the cycle of rumination that so often prevents us from falling asleep.

The 5 Best Bedtime Poses

Try these five poses in sequence. Move slowly, and remember that comfort is more important than achieving a "perfect" shape.

1. Viparita Karani (Legs-Up-The-Wall): Lie on your back and extend your legs up a wall. This pose improves circulation and drains fluid from the lower legs. It is incredibly grounding for the nervous system.

2. Balasana (Child’s Pose): Kneel, sit back on your heels, and fold your torso over your thighs with your forehead touching the floor. This provides a gentle release for the lower back and shoulders.

3. Supta Matsyendrasana (Supine Spinal Twist): Lying on your back, bring your knees to your chest and then gently lower them to one side while keeping your shoulders pinned to the mat. It helps release tension in the spine and mid-back.

4. Supta Baddha Konasana (Reclining Butterfly): Lie on your back, bring the soles of your feet together, and let your knees fall outward. Place pillows under your knees for extra support. This opens the hips—a primary storage area for stress.

5. Savasana (Corpse Pose): Simply lie flat on your back with arms and legs slightly spread. Focus entirely on releasing every muscle in your body, from your toes to your jaw.

Consistency and Breathing

A bedtime yoga routine does not need to be an hour-long ordeal to be effective. In fact, consistency is far more valuable than intensity. Even a 10-minute session performed consistently every night is superior to a 60-minute session performed once a week.

During these poses, prioritize "diaphragmatic breathing." Inhale deeply through your nose, allowing your belly to expand, and exhale slowly through your mouth. This type of deep breathing is the fastest way to manually override a stress response and induce a state of physiological calm.

Key Takeaways

  • Bedtime yoga helps shift the nervous system from a high-stress active state into a restorative "rest and digest" mode.
  • Focus on holds of 2–5 minutes per pose to truly allow muscles and fascia to release tension.
  • Practice deep diaphragmatic breathing to enhance the calming effects of the poses.

Safety and Preparation

Safety in bedtime yoga means respecting your body's current level of flexibility. Never force a stretch, and use props like blankets, bolsters, or firm pillows to bring the floor "up" to you if needed. If you suffer from severe back pain, consult with a physical therapist to ensure these movements are appropriate for your specific condition.

Finally, keep the environment conducive to sleep. Dim the lights, put your phone in another room, and perhaps use a calming scent like lavender. Your yoga practice should be the final event of the day, signaling to your brain that the "active" portion of your life has concluded.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best time to practice bedtime yoga?

The ideal time to practice is 30 to 60 minutes before bed. This timeframe allows your heart rate to slow down and signals to your nervous system that it is time to transition into a rest state.

Do I need to be flexible to practice yoga?

Absolutely not. Yoga is about functional movement and breath, not gymnastics. You can modify every pose with pillows or blankets to suit your current flexibility level.

Can yoga help if I have chronic pain?

Gentle, restorative yoga can help alleviate chronic tension. However, always consult with your physical therapist or physician before starting a new routine if you have a medical condition.
