Place Your Jade Plant Here: The Feng Shui Trick for Wealth & Happiness

By James Carter Senior Editor
Published: May 21, 2026 Updated: May 21, 2026 6 Min Read

The jade plant (*Crassula ovata*) has long been celebrated in both interior design and traditional Chinese Feng Shui. Known as the "Money Plant" or "Lucky Plant," its distinct, succulent, coin-shaped leaves are widely believed to vibrate with energy that attracts wealth, prosperity, and good fortune. However, simply owning the plant isn't enough; placement is considered the secret to unlocking these benefits.

In the practice of Feng Shui, your environment is a mirror of your life. By curating your space, you can guide the flow of 'Chi' (energy). Placing a jade plant in the right quadrant of your home or workspace is thought to act as a magnet for positive financial outcomes and general happiness.

Why the Jade Plant is Special

It is not coincidental that the jade plant is synonymous with wealth. In Feng Shui, the shape of leaves matters significantly. The succulent, round leaves of the *Crassula ovata* are reminiscent of jade coins, which were historically used as currency in China. This resemblance has cemented its status as a physical manifestation of wealth accumulation.

Beyond symbolism, jade plants are hardy, long-lived, and grow slowly. This longevity is perceived as a representation of stable, growing, and enduring prosperity, rather than a "get-rich-quick" scheme. It is a plant that rewards patient care—much like building long-term financial stability.

Where to Place It for Wealth

According to the Bagua—the Feng Shui energy map of your space—the southeast corner of a home or office is designated as the "Wealth and Prosperity" area. This is the optimal spot to place your jade plant.

Placing it here is believed to activate the energy of growth and abundance. If the southeast corner of your living room or office is cluttered, clearing it and adding a vibrant, healthy jade plant can improve the energy flow. Alternatively, placing the plant near your front entrance is also recommended, as it is thought to greet incoming positive energy (and potential opportunities) as you enter your home.

Essential Care Tips

In Feng Shui, a dying or sickly plant is considered "dead energy," which can stifle growth. Therefore, keeping your jade plant healthy is a vital part of the practice. Fortunately, jade plants are beginner-friendly succulents.

Lighting: These plants crave bright, indirect sunlight. A south-facing window is usually ideal. Without enough light, the stems will become leggy and weak, which is not the image of strength you want to cultivate.

Watering: The golden rule is "less is more." Only water when the soil feels completely dry to the touch (usually every 7–10 days). Overwatering is the fastest way to kill a jade plant, leading to root rot that drains the vitality from your home.

Common Myths vs. Reality

While Feng Shui provides a beautiful framework for organizing our lives, it is important to balance tradition with reality. The jade plant is a fantastic addition to your decor and a great conversation starter, but it should not be viewed as a substitute for sound financial planning.

The "trick" of Feng Shui is often about mindfulness. By consciously placing a symbol of wealth in your office, you are setting a daily intention to focus on your financial goals. The plant doesn't conjure money out of thin air, but the mindfulness it encourages can lead to better decision-making and a more positive approach to your career and finances.

Key Takeaways

  • Place your jade plant in the southeast corner of your office or home to activate 'Wealth' energy.
  • Keep the plant healthy—sickly plants can represent stagnant or negative energy.
  • Use the plant as a physical anchor for your daily financial and personal intentions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of jade plant is best for attracting wealth?

The Crassula ovata, often called the "Money Plant," is favored in Feng Shui because its round, coin-shaped leaves symbolize the accumulation of wealth and prosperity.

How often should I water my jade plant?

Jade plants are succulents. Water them only when the top two inches of soil are completely dry. Overwatering is the most common cause of root rot.

Can I place my jade plant in a spot with low light?

While jade plants can tolerate lower light, they tend to become weak and leggy. They do best in bright, indirect light, so aim for a spot that receives plenty of natural illumination.
